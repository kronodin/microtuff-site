Solar Fundamentals

Photovoltaic (PV) systems convert sunlight into electricity using semiconductor materials. The core equation:

Watts = Volts × Amps
Daily Wh = Panel Watts × Sun Hours × System Efficiency (0.75)
Jamaica Solar Irradiance: Average 5.0–5.5 peak sun hours across Manchester Parish. A 400W panel produces: 400W × 5.5h × 0.75 = 1,650Wh/day

Solar & Electrical Glossary

Simple definitions for the most common terms you will see on solar labels, invoices, and JPS net-billing paperwork.

Watt (W)

Unit of power. A 100W bulb uses 100 watts while it is on.

Kilowatt (kW)

1,000 watts. A 5 kW solar system produces 5,000 watts at peak sun.

Kilowatt-hour (kWh)

Unit of energy. Running a 1 kW heater for 1 hour = 1 kWh. JPS bills in kWh.

Volt (V)

Electrical pressure. Common solar systems use 12V, 24V, or 48V battery banks.

Ampere / Amp (A)

Flow rate of current. Wiring size and fuses are chosen based on amps.

Amp-hour (Ah)

Battery capacity. A 100Ah battery can deliver 5A for 20 hours.

PSI

Pounds per square inch. Used for water pressure and some pipe fittings.

BTU

British Thermal Unit. Heat energy measurement; relevant for water heating sizing.

Peak Sun Hours

Hours per day of sun intensity equal to 1,000 W/m². Jamaica averages 5.0–5.5.

Inverter

Converts DC battery power to AC for household appliances.

Charge Controller

Regulates charging from panels to batteries to prevent overcharge.

Grid-Tied

System connected to JPS lines. Allows net billing or net metering where excess is sold back.
